Ghent& its region — Gravensteen · the Ghent Altarpiece · Graslei · Patershol · the Leie

53 sights and 55 places to eat across Ghent and its region — from the moated Gravensteen and St Bavo’s Cathedral with the Ghent Altarpiece (Van Eyck) to the Graslei/Korenlei quays, the Belfry, the medieval Patershol, SMAK/MSK and the Werregarenstraat graffiti alley, out to Sint-Martens-Latem and the Leie. A food canon all its own: Gentse waterzooi and stoverij, the purple cuberdon (neuzekes) carts, Tierenteyn mustard, the Gruut city brewery and the boot of beer at Dulle Griet — plus Europe’s veggie capital. How ranks workMust see, worth the detour and deep cut are my judgement, not any source's. They are graded within each region — a must-see in the suburbs is not competing with a must-see downtown, so filtering to must-see still gives you a workable day in each area. Roughly: must-see means you would regret missing it; deep cut means a plaque, a private house, a two-minute stop, or something so seasonal you need luck. The count on the right of each entry shows how many separate sources named it, which is a different and more objective signal — sort by that if you would rather trust the crowd than me.
Hours, admission and opening days change — verify before you drive.
A few listed places are seasonal, tour-only, or open one day a year. Those are flagged.
