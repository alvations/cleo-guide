Field guide · Tokyo (東京), sourced

Tokyoward by ward · Jōnan · Jōsai · Jōhoku · Jōtō · Tama · Kantō day trips

224 sights and 118 places to eat across Tokyo, organised the way Tokyoites think about the city — the big special wards (Chiyoda, Chūō, Minato, Shinjuku, Shibuya, Taitō, Sumida-Kōtō) as their own areas, the rest by the old compass terms Jōnan, Jōsai, Jōhoku and Jōtō, then the Tama hills and the Kantō day-trip ring (Kamakura, Hakone, Nikkō, Kawagoe, Fuji Five Lakes, Yokohama). The food list starts from what is Tokyo's own — Kanda soba, Edomae sushi and unagi, monjayaki, dozeu loach, yōshoku omurice, tonkatsu and shōyu ramen — with the Michelin bench and century-old shinise beside each other. Every place is traceable to its source. Switch modes below, filter by area, collection or cuisine, and tick anything to build your own list, then export it to Google or Apple Maps.

How ranks workMust see, worth the detour and deep cut are my judgement, not any source's. They are graded within each region — a must-see in the suburbs is not competing with a must-see downtown, so filtering to must-see still gives you a workable day in each area. Roughly: must-see means you would regret missing it; deep cut means a plaque, a private house, a two-minute stop, or something so seasonal you need luck. The count on the right of each entry shows how many separate sources named it, which is a different and more objective signal — sort by that if you would rather trust the crowd than me.
