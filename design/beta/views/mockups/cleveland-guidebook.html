Downtown & The Flats

Top picks 10

  1. 1Chess Collection — Cleveland Public Library MAIN Library

    Must see325 Superior Ave NE · Main Building, 3rd floor, John G. White Special Collections

    <strong>This is the right building.</strong> The 1925 Main Library on Superior Avenue — not a branch, not the Louis Stokes Wing next door. Go in the Main Building, take the marble staircase to the third floor, and pass through the John Griswold White Exhibition Corridor (look for owls in the wrought-iron cases) into Special Collections.<br><br>Inside is the John G. White Chess and Checkers Collection: the largest chess collection in the world. Over 35,000 items going back to the 12th century — one-of-a-kind sets from every continent, rare manuscripts, Bobby Fischer score sheets, Lasker medals, the most comprehensive tournament records anywhere, and two death masks of Gisela Kahn Gresser, nine-time US women's champion. White was a Cleveland attorney who collected from 1870 until his death in 1928, buying up entire European and American chess libraries; the library has kept collecting since. It has been on this floor since the doors opened.

    Look for: Bring photo ID such as a driving licence. It is a closed research collection — you request items and are shown one at a time, so email special.collections@cpl.org ahead if you want something specific pulled. Closed Sunday. <br><br><strong>Note:</strong> News 5's list prints the address as 525 Superior. That is wrong — the Main Library is at 325 Superior Ave.

    Recommended by Asked for by nameNews 5 Cleveland

  2. 2The Arcade (Old Arcade)

    Must see401 Euclid Ave

    Five-story glass-roofed atrium built in 1890 as America's first indoor shopping centre, modelled on Milan's Galleria and financed in part by Rockefeller. Top three floors are now a Hyatt. A Superman film was shot here.

    Look for: Stand at the far end and look up the full height. Grab Pizza 216 while you're in.

    Recommended by News 5 ClevelandThere She Goes Again

  3. 3Terminal Tower Observation Deck

    Must see50 Public Square, 42nd floor

    The 42nd floor of a 52-story tower that was briefly the world's second-tallest. Views across the bridges, the Cuyahoga and Lake Erie. Small and dated in a way that's part of the appeal.

    Look for: Book online in advance — no walk-ups. Check in at the ground-floor desk, ride to 32, transfer to 42. Around $5–6. Weekends only.

    Recommended by News 5 ClevelandThere She Goes Again

  4. 4GE Chandelier, Playhouse Square

    Must seeE 14th St at Euclid Ave

    The largest outdoor chandelier in North America, hung 44 feet above Euclid Avenue: 4,200 crystals, each placed by hand at roughly twenty minutes apiece. It anchors Playhouse Square, the largest performing arts centre in the United States outside Lincoln Center — five restored 1920s theatres saved from demolition by a volunteer campaign.

    Look for: Free and lit every night. Go after dark. Parking nearby is genuinely difficult.

    Recommended by Atlas ObscuraAdded by the guide

  5. 5Slyman's Restaurant and Deli

    Must see3106 St Clair Ave NE

    The corned beef sandwich Cleveland argues about — absurdly overstacked, and a fixture of national food TV for decades. Also the best pastrami on toasted rye in the city, by a number of accounts.

    Look for: Monday to Friday, 7am–2:30pm. Closed both weekend days. Go early; the queue is real. Roughly $25 a sandwich now.

    Recommended by Bourdain / TVAdded by the guide

  6. 6Soldiers' & Sailors' Monument

    Must see3 Public Square

    Civil War memorial with a free museum inside — bronze reliefs, marble busts, knowledgeable volunteers. Beneath it runs a labyrinth of tunnels that opens to the public one day a year.

    Look for: Ask at the desk whether a tunnel tour is scheduled. Interior alone: 20–30 minutes. Free.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  7. 7Free Stamp

    Must seeWillard Park, 601 Lakeside Ave E

    Claes Oldenburg and Coosje van Bruggen's giant rubber stamp, tipped on its side, reading FREE. Roughly 28 × 26 × 49 feet. Commissioned for a corporate plaza, rejected, then exiled here — arguably the better ending.

    Recommended by There She Goes Again

  8. 8Rock & Roll Hall of Fame

    Must see1100 E 9th St

    Pei's glass pyramid on the lakefront, five floors of exhibits. Even if you're not a devotee the history of how the music got made — and the DJs who took the risks — carries it.

    Look for: Buy ahead. Budget three hours minimum; six if you're thorough.

    Recommended by There She Goes Again

  9. 9Steamship William G. Mather

    Must see601 Erieside Ave, North Coast Harbor

    A 618-foot Great Lakes freighter tied up beside the Science Center, open stem to stern — engine room, pilot house, crew quarters and the panelled guest staterooms. The deck gives one of the better skyline views in the city.

    Look for: Closed Monday, and seasonal. Included with Great Lakes Science Center membership.

    Recommended by Atlas Obscura

  10. 10Hope Memorial Bridge & the Guardians of Traffic

    Must seeHope Memorial Bridge, over the Cuyahoga

    Eight art deco sandstone giants, each 43 feet tall, holding vehicles from a hay wagon to a 1930s truck — Henry Hering's Guardians of Traffic, carved into the bridge pylons in 1932. The baseball team took its name from them. Pedestrian walkways run both sides.

    Look for: Walk it rather than drive it. Best light is late afternoon from the west end.

    Recommended by Added by the guide

University Circle & East Side

University Circle & East Side holds 59 places in this guide: 43 sights and 14 places to eat or drink. Its must-sees start with Haserot Angel, Lake View Cemetery and Superior Phở. The source that names the most of them is News 5 Cleveland (30). 2 places here have closed; they stay at the end of the chapter, flagged.

Top picks 13

  1. 1Haserot Angel, Lake View Cemetery

    Must see12316 Euclid Ave · Section 9, Lot 14

    Formally "The Angel of Death Victorious." A life-size bronze on the Haserot family plot, holding an extinguished torch upside down. Decades of oxidation have left black streaks running from her eyes down her neck, so she appears to weep. Herman Matzen sculpted her; canning magnate Francis Haserot commissioned her after his wife died in 1919.

    Look for: Easy to miss — just off the road under trees. Grab a map at the gate; nearest marked landmark is the Hanna Mausoleum.

    Recommended by Atlas ObscuraNews 5 ClevelandThere She Goes Again

  2. 2Superior Phở

    Must see3030 Superior Ave E #105, Asiatown

    Vietnamese-owned and family-run in Asiatown, and by consensus the best phở in the city. The bone broth simmers 24 hours from a recipe passed down through the family; the owner is known to throw out an entire vat that doesn't meet standard. Fourteen versions of phở, plus bánh mì, bún thịt nướng, summer rolls and proper Vietnamese coffee.

    Look for: Enter through Golden Plaza, a nondescript mini-mall with free parking. Closed Monday. Do not confuse it with Number One Pho two doors down — people mix them up constantly.

    Recommended by The Infatuation ClevelandFreshwater ClevelandCleveland Magazine

  3. 3Li Wah — Cantonese dim sum carts

    Must seeAsia Plaza, 2999 Payne Ave #102

    <strong>Cantonese.</strong> Freshwater calls it the best dim sum in Cleveland and it is still the only place running proper steel carts at weekend lunch — har gow, siu mai, chicken feet, roast duck, rice rolls, circling the room. Hundreds of people pack it on Saturday and Sunday.

    Look for: Carts run weekend lunch only; come before 1pm or you get the menu instead. Some off-cart dishes draw mixed reviews — stick to dim sum and roast meats.

    Recommended by Freshwater ClevelandCleveland MagazineCleveland Scene

  4. 4Percy Skuy Collection, Dittrick Medical History Center

    Must see11000 Euclid Ave, Allen Memorial Medical Library

    The largest and most comprehensive collection of historical contraceptives in the world — over a thousand objects arranged chronologically across a millennium. Beaver testicles, crocodile dung, mule earwax, spider carcasses, Mexican yams, Goodyear rubbers, IUDs. Alongside sit the texts: Casanova recommending a hollowed half-lemon, and 1580 BCE instructions involving ground dates, acacia and honey. The wider Dittrick galleries on medical history are excellent in their own right.

    Look for: Very limited hours and they should drive your whole day. Free. Metered street parking.

    Recommended by Atlas ObscuraNews 5 Cleveland

  5. 5Lake View Cemetery (whole grounds)

    Must see12316 Euclid Ave

    280 acres, 100,000 graves, and the best architecture walk in the city: the James A. Garfield Memorial and tomb, Wade Memorial Chapel with a Louis Tiffany interior, and mausoleums throughout. Climb the Garfield tower for a downtown view.

    Look for: Maps at the gate, main office and Garfield Monument. Bikes allowed. A trolley tour runs at 1pm — call to book. Open every day of the year.

    Recommended by There She Goes AgainNews 5 Cleveland

  6. 6Wonton Gourmet & BBQ

    Must see3211 Payne Ave, Asiatown

    <strong>Cantonese / Hong Kong-style.</strong> Freshwater rates it the best Chinese restaurant in Asiatown and says some would extend that to all of Northeast Ohio. Roast duck and char siu hanging in the window, chive pot stickers, shrimp wonton noodle soup, three-flavour dumpling soup, salt-baked shrimp, soy sauce chicken, beef chow fun. Full-colour photos of nearly every dish cover three of the four walls.

    Look for: Closed Tuesday. Cash-friendly, cheap, and not remotely fancy.

    Recommended by Freshwater ClevelandCleveland Scene

  7. 7Cleveland Museum of Art

    Must see11150 East Blvd

    One of the largest museums in the country, free to enter. The 1916 building, East and West wings and North building wrap a glass-roofed atrium. Armour court, Egyptian galleries, strong Asian collection upstairs in the North building.

    Look for: Enter via Wade Park and the South entrance for the approach across the lagoon. Special exhibitions are ticketed; the permanent collection isn't. Closed Monday.

    Recommended by There She Goes Again

  8. 8Lucy, at the Cleveland Museum of Natural History

    Must see1 Wade Oval Dr

    The Australopithecus afarensis skeleton — the oldest discovered link in the chain of human evolution, and Cleveland's most famous resident. Balto the sled dog is here too, taxidermied and on display. Live animals outside, including a bald eagle.

    Look for: Planetarium and 3D films cost extra. Attached garage. Two hours minimum. Closed Monday.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  9. 9Quintana's Speakeasy

    Must see2200 S Taylor Rd, Cleveland Heights

    Go to the back door of the barbershop and spa. Press the buzzer. Say you're there to see a man about a horse. Up the stairs, turn right, find the bookshelf, pull the correct book — the wall opens onto a 1920s bar.

    Look for: Closed Sunday and Monday. Small capacity; the attic room is the quiet one. They run cocktail classes.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  10. 10Wade Memorial Chapel

    Must seeLake View Cemetery, 12316 Euclid Ave

    From outside, an unremarkable cemetery chapel. Inside, a Louis Tiffany interior: a monumental stained-glass window and glass mosaic murals running the length of both walls, executed by an all-female workshop. It was the first place in Cleveland outside a private home to have electric light. The bronze doors weigh two tons and swing on a finger.

    Look for: Open daily 10–4, free, and there is usually a volunteer docent inside who will tell you far more than any placard. Do this in the same visit as the Haserot Angel and Garfield's tomb.

    Recommended by Atlas Obscura

  11. 11The Thinker

    Must seeCleveland Museum of Art, 11150 East Blvd

    A Rodin cast at the museum's south entrance, and one of very few in the world deliberately left damaged. A bomb detonated at its base in 1970 — attributed to the Weather Underground — tore off the lower legs and pedestal. The museum chose not to restore it.

    Recommended by Atlas Obscura

  12. 12Severance Music Center

    Must see11001 Euclid Ave, University Circle

    Home of the Cleveland Orchestra — by most reckonings one of the finest orchestras in the world — in a 1931 hall whose art deco and Egyptian Revival interior is worth the ticket on its own.

    Look for: Season runs roughly September to May; in summer the orchestra decamps to Blossom. Metered street parking is often free in the evening — check ParkMobile before paying $20 to the garage.

    Recommended by Added by the guide

  13. 13Presti's Bakery

    Must see12101 Mayfield Rd, Little Italy

    A century-old Italian bakery and the anchor of Little Italy. Cannoli, sfogliatelle, lady locks, Linzer cookies, plus savoury Stromboli and artichoke pizza. Take a number or you will get cut in line.

    Recommended by Added by the guide

West Side, Ohio City & Tremont

West Side, Ohio City & Tremont holds 52 places in this guide: 30 sights and 17 places to eat or drink. Its must-sees start with West Side Market and Buckland Museum of Witchcraft & Magick. The source that names the most of them is News 5 Cleveland (19). 5 places here have closed; they stay at the end of the chapter, flagged.

Top picks 14

  1. 1West Side Market

    Must see1979 W 25th St, Ohio City

    A hundred stalls under a 1912 barrel-vaulted hall with a clock tower — butchers, bakers, fishmongers, cheesemongers, spice sellers. Kielbasa in every form: smoked, fresh, double-smoked, Hungarian, Polish. One stand is called the Pork Chop Shop. Food has been sold on or beside this site since 1840. Look for the steer's head carved into the northern pediment, and go up to the balcony for the view down the hall.

    Look for: Bring cash — most stalls don't take cards. First hour of parking free; park toward the back near the beer garden. The outdoor produce arcade is a separate building and a harder sell than the main hall. Closed Tuesday and Thursday.

    Recommended by Atlas ObscuraThere She Goes AgainNews 5 Cleveland

  2. 2Buckland Museum of Witchcraft & Magick

    Must see2155 Broadview Rd

    Raymond Buckland — leader of what became the Long Island Coven — started this collection in a Bay Shore basement in the 1960s. It followed him to New Hampshire, through legal disputes in New Orleans, into storage in Columbus, and finally here. Much of it originally belonged to Gerald Gardner, father of Gardnerian Wicca, including his besom. Also: Sybil Leek's crystal ball, Egyptian ushabtis, a carved mandrake root, Crowley material, rare Ouija memorabilia, and a demon in a box that Buckland captured in the 1970s with help from a ceremonial magician.

    Look for: Ten minutes from downtown. Parking behind the building and on the street. Photos without flash; the curator asks for no video. Small enough for an hour, dense enough to want longer.

    Recommended by Atlas ObscuraNews 5 Cleveland

  3. 3A Christmas Story House & Museum

    Must see3159 W 11th St, Tremont

    The actual exterior house from the 1983 film, restored inside to match the movie, plus a museum of original props and costumes across the street. The tour covers why it was shot in Cleveland at all.

    Look for: House interior is tour-only; tickets at the gift shop, departures every 30 minutes from 10:15am. Budget two hours. You can rent the house and sleep in Ralphie's bedroom.

    Recommended by There She Goes AgainNews 5 Cleveland

  4. 4Larder Delicatessen and Bakery

    Must see1455 W 29th St, Hingetown

    Central and Eastern European deli technique crossed with koji fermentation — the Japanese fungus used to fast-forward curing, which gives their pastrami a salt-sweet umami layer nothing else in the city has. Menu changes daily and is announced on Instagram. James Beard-recognised.

    Look for: Hours have contracted sharply — recent listings show Saturday only. Check Instagram the morning you plan to go.

    Recommended by The Infatuation ClevelandAdded by the guide

    <strong>Bourdain's favourite stop in Cleveland</strong> — chicken paprikash, stuffed cabbage, head cheese and boiled pig's head, served cafeteria-style. “This might be the greatest establishment ever,” he said. A James Beard America's Classic. It closed after 100 years in the same family. Listed so you know it is gone rather than wondering.

    Look for: Closed permanently. Marie's on St Clair is the closest living equivalent.

    Recommended by Bourdain / TV

Suburbs, Metroparks & Day Trips

Suburbs, Metroparks & Day Trips holds 41 places in this guide: 34 sights and 5 places to eat or drink. Its must-sees start with Brandywine Falls and Virginia Kendall Ledges Overlook. The source that names the most of them is News 5 Cleveland (26). 2 places here have closed; they stay at the end of the chapter, flagged.

Top picks 10

  1. 1Brandywine Falls

    Must see8176 Brandywine Rd, Northfield

    A 65-foot waterfall reached by a boardwalk from the car park, viewable from above or from the gorge floor. Different in every season.

    Look for: Stairs at both viewing levels — not fully accessible. Gets very busy; go early.

    Recommended by News 5 ClevelandThere She Goes Again

  2. 2Virginia Kendall Ledges Overlook

    Must see405 Truxell Rd, Peninsula

    Sandstone ledges, mossy crevices and a west-facing overlook that is the best sunset in the national park. Mysterious carvings are scattered through the rock.

    Recommended by News 5 ClevelandThere She Goes Again

  3. 3Beck Center for the Arts

    Must see17801 Detroit Ave, Lakewood, OH 44107

    The largest performing-arts and arts-education center on Cleveland's West Shore — a nonprofit founded in 1931 (as Lakewood Little Theatre) that entertains and educates 65,000+ people a year across two stages on a 3.5-acre Detroit Ave campus. Professional theater, galleries, and classes; documented by the Encyclopedia of Cleveland History and listed by TourismOhio.

    Recommended by Encyclopedia of Cleveland HistoryOhio.org / TourismOhio

  4. 4Rocky River Reservation & Fort Hill Stairs

    Must see24000 Valley Pkwy, North Olmsted, OH 44070

    A Cleveland Metroparks jewel of the Emerald Necklace winding along the Rocky River — over five miles of trails to river's edge, wetlands, ponds and ancient Native American earthworks, plus the Fort Hill Stairs (155 steps rising 90 ft to a bluff-top view). The Rocky River Nature Center displays the prehistoric fish Dunkleosteus and the kid-favorite HideAway Hollow oak.

    Recommended by Cleveland MetroparksCleveland Scene

  5. 5Huntington Reservation & Beach

    Must seeLake Rd at Wolf Rd, Bay Village, OH 44140

    A ~100-acre Cleveland Metroparks lakefront reservation in Bay Village with a swimming beach on Lake Erie, three miles of trails through beach-forest-meadow habitats, and the landmark 1927 Huntington water tower with a play space and snack spot. Home to two Metroparks affiliates (Lake Erie Nature & Science Center and BAYarts).

    Recommended by Cleveland MetroparksOhio.org / TourismOhio

  6. 6Cain Park

    Must see14591 Superior Rd, Cleveland Heights, OH 44118

    One of the oldest municipally owned arts parks in the U.S., conceived in 1938 by Heights drama teacher Dr. Dina Rees Evans and Mayor Frank C. Cain — the covered 2,782-seat Evans Amphitheater, the Alma Theater, the Feinberg gallery, and the July Cain Park Arts Festival, a nationally top-rated juried outdoor art fair. Entrances at Lee Rd and Superior Park Dr.

    Recommended by Ohio.org / TourismOhiocleveland.com / The Plain Dealer

  7. 7Cuyahoga Valley National Park

    Must seeBoston Mill Visitor Center, 6947 Riverview Rd, Peninsula

    A full national park half an hour from downtown that a surprising number of Ohioans have never visited. Start at Boston Mill for maps and rangers.

    Look for: With one afternoon and a car: Brandywine Falls, then sunset from the Ledges Overlook.

    Recommended by There She Goes Again

  8. 8Squire's Castle

    Must see2844 River Rd, North Chagrin Reservation, Willoughby Hills

    A roofless stone gatehouse — all that was ever built of Feargus Squire's country estate — standing free and open on a wide lawn. Hollow inside, thick with urban myth, and beautiful in fog.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  9. 9Holden Arboretum & Stebbins Gulch

    Must see9550 Sperry Rd, Kirtland

    Stebbins Gulch is Holden's crown jewel — a ravine of cascades and waterfalls reachable only on a guided tour. The wider arboretum has a canopy walk and an emergent tower.

    Look for: Gulch access is guided only and must be booked. Closed Mondays.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  10. 10Tita Flora's

    Must see6531 Brecksville Rd, Independence

    Filipino, and the highest-rated Asian restaurant in the Cleveland area by diner score. Lechon pork belly, tofu sisig, pancit bihon, ginataang, halo-halo. Filipino reviewers write about walking in and feeling homesick — which is the review that matters.

    Look for: Closed Monday. Worth the twenty-minute drive south.

    Recommended by Added by the guide

See 25

  1. 11Cedar Lee Theatre

    Worth the detour2163 Lee Rd, Cleveland Heights, OH 44118

    Cleveland's premier arthouse cinema and the anchor of the Cedar Lee district — open since 1925 and now a six-screen Cleveland Cinemas house for indie, foreign and documentary films, famous for its monthly midnight 'Rocky Horror Picture Show.'

    Recommended by Cleveland Scenecleveland.com / The Plain Dealer

  2. 12Worden's Ledges

    Worth the detourWordens Ledges Loop Trail, Hinckley

    Faces and iconography carved into sandstone ledges in the 1940s by Noble Stuart, who did it in the woods his late wife had inherited. George Washington, Jefferson, a sphinx, and Ty Cobb.

    Look for: Trail is marked with a raccoon-head blaze and crossed by bridle trails — easy to end up on the wrong one.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  3. 13Whipp's Ledges

    Worth the detourHinckley Reservation

    Rock ledges, small caves and free-climbing scrambles — closer than Hocking Hills and far less crowded. Swim at the spillway after.

    Look for: Steeper and rockier than it looks; trails are poorly marked on the way down.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  4. 14Henry Church Rock

    Worth the detourSouth Chagrin Reservation

    A blacksmith and self-taught artist carved Native American imagery into this riverside boulder in 1885, working at night by lantern.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  5. 15Mill Creek Falls Overlook

    Worth the detourGarfield Park Reservation

    The tallest waterfall in Cuyahoga County at 48 feet. Its power drove the mills that helped build the city.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  6. 16Viaduct Park

    Worth the detourWillis St, Bedford Reservation

    A short walk down into the valley gets you Tinker's Creek Falls, mill ruins, wildflowers, trains and observation platforms — a lot packed into a small park.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  7. 17Skyline Overlook Trailhead

    Worth the detourWest Creek Reservation, 1969 W Ridgewood Dr, Parma

    The downtown skyline seen from the suburbs — about fifteen minutes up a well-kept trail from the car park.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  8. 18Observatory Park

    Worth the detour10610 Clay St, Montville

    An 1,100-acre certified dark-sky park with a Nassau telescope programme, a scale-model solar system, a pyramid and a stone circle. Come for a meteor shower.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  9. 19Gardenview Horticultural Park

    Worth the detour16711 Pearl Rd, Strongsville

    Sixteen acres of rare plants in a cottage-style landscape, half-returned to nature for want of hands. An unexpected green island in a stretch of retail.

    Look for: Small admission. Closed Mondays.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  10. 20Polka Hall of Fame

    Worth the detour605 E 222nd St, Euclid

    Free, in Euclid's historic old city hall, and a room full of accordions. Frankie Yankovic, Johnny Pecon, and the Slovenian roots of Cleveland-style polka. The Softball Hall of Fame is next door.

    Look for: Wednesday and Saturday mornings only.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  11. 21Fairport Harbor Marine Museum & Lighthouse

    Worth the detour129 Second St, Fairport Harbor

    Sixty-nine steps to a walkway around the lantern and a view over the harbour, plus a Great Lakes shipping museum below. Ask about the ghost cat.

    Look for: Wednesday, Friday, weekends. Around $10.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  12. 22Chagrin Falls Popcorn Shop

    Worth the detour53 N Main St, Chagrin Falls

    Seventy years of popcorn, ice cream and unclassifiable sweets in a tiny shop that sits directly above the falls the village is named for.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  13. 23Mapleside Farms

    Worth the detour294 Pearl Rd, Brunswick

    An orchard on a ridge above the valley. Go for a sunset concert; in autumn it becomes a full festival with a super slide and corn mazes.

    Look for: Heavily seasonal — much of the year it's events-only.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  14. 24Cleveland Orchestra at Blossom Music Center

    Worth the detour1145 W Steels Corners Rd, Cuyahoga Falls

    One of the world's great orchestras, outdoors on a wooded hillside, every summer. Lawn tickets are cheap and the acoustics carry.

    Look for: Summer season only. Traffic leaving the car parks is a known ordeal.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  15. 25Cascade Park

    Worth the detour387 Furnace St, Elyria

    The Black River drops through a gorge in the middle of Elyria and West Falls is the headline — one of the best waterfalls in Ohio. Paved path to the falls if the trails are too steep, plus rock formations along the river.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  16. 26A Bench By The Road

    Worth the detourCorner of Main & Lorain Streets, Oberlin

    One of only about twenty benches worldwide placed by the Toni Morrison Society to mark the lives of enslaved Africans — a response to Morrison's remark that there was no memorial bench by the road. Morrison was born in nearby Lorain.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  17. 27Cuyahoga Valley Scenic Railroad

    Worth the detourPeninsula Depot, 1630 Mill St W, Peninsula

    A heritage train through the national park, run largely by volunteer conductors who narrate as you go. There is a dome car. You can also put a bike on board, ride one way and cycle the towpath back.

    Look for: Book ahead — peak-season and autumn-colour runs sell out.

    Recommended by Atlas Obscura

  18. 28Hale Farm & Village

    Worth the detour2686 Oak Hill Rd, Bath

    A living-history village inside the national park — working blacksmith, glassblower and potter, period buildings moved here from across the Western Reserve, and costumed interpreters at weekends in season.

    Look for: Wednesday to Sunday, seasonal. Pairs naturally with Brandywine Falls and the scenic railroad.

    Recommended by Added by the guide

  19. 32Judy's Jungle

    Deep cut8665 Station St, Mentor

    A sprawling garden of fibreglass animals hiding behind an HVAC company's car park. Overgrown and in need of paint, which honestly improves it. Free.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  20. 33Preston's H.O.P.E. Playground

    Deep cut26001 S Woodland Rd, Beachwood

    The largest fully accessible playground in Northeast Ohio, built as a village of small buildings and inspired by a boy born with Spinal Muscular Atrophy.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  21. 34Sims Park & Lakefront Trail

    Deep cut23131 Lakeshore Blvd, Euclid

    Edgewater isn't the only lakefront park in the county. A mansion, a beach cove, a fishing pier and a new jogging path, with far fewer people.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  22. 35Lakefront Lodge

    Deep cut30525 Lakeshore Blvd, Willowick

    A clifftop park with swings, picnic tables and an underrated sunset. Pack something from Alesci's at Shoregate on the way.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  23. 36Gravity Hill

    Deep cutKing Memorial Rd, north of Little Mountain Rd, Kirtland Hills

    An optical illusion of terrain: the road appears to run uphill, and a car in neutral rolls the way it shouldn't.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  24. 37East Falls Riverwalk

    Deep cut18 West Ave, Elyria

    The other set of falls in Elyria, with a boardwalk right along the water. News 5's entire write-up was: look at it. LOOK. AT. IT.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

  25. 38Amherst Veterans Mural

    Deep cut248 Park Ave, Amherst

    A large painted veterans' memorial wall, and there is more to it than the photographs show.

    Recommended by News 5 Cleveland

    Norton's country roadhouse for Barberton-style chicken — juicy lard-fried birds with an especially craveable crust, the traditional hot sauce and sides, in a wood-paneled room regulars swear edges out the rest. About 40 min south.

    Recommended by Fox 8 Cleveland

    Deep cut15607 Detroit Ave, Lakewood

    A tiny, well-worn Lakewood neighborhood pub whose Lenten Friday fish and Lake Erie perch fries are a west-side ritual — cash-friendly, unpretentious, packed. About 15 min west.
