1C
Must see· Downtown & The Flats

Chess Collection — Cleveland Public Library MAIN Library

This is the right building. The 1925 Main Library on Superior Avenue — not a branch, not the Louis Stokes Wing next door. Go in the Main Building, take the marble staircase to the third floor, and pass through the John Griswold White Exhibition Corridor (look for owls in the wrought-iron cases) into Special Collections. Inside is the John G. White Chess and Checkers Collection: the largest chess collection in the world. Over 35,000 items going back to the 12th century — one-of-a-kind sets from every continent, rare manuscripts, Bobby Fischer score sheets, Lasker medals, the most comprehensive tournament records anywhere, and two death masks of Gisela Kahn Gresser, nine-time US women's champion. White was a Cleveland attorney who collected from 1870 until his death in 1928, buying up entire European and American chess libraries; the library has kept collecting since. It has been on this floor since the doors opened.

1H
Must see· University Circle & East Side

Haserot Angel, Lake View Cemetery

Formally "The Angel of Death Victorious." A life-size bronze on the Haserot family plot, holding an extinguished torch upside down. Decades of oxidation have left black streaks running from her eyes down her neck, so she appears to weep. Herman Matzen sculpted her; canning magnate Francis Haserot commissioned her after his wife died in 1919.

Recommended byAtlas ObscuraNews 5 Cleveland #5+1
2S
Must see· University Circle & East Side · Vietnamese

Superior Phở

Vietnamese-owned and family-run in Asiatown, and by consensus the best phở in the city. The bone broth simmers 24 hours from a recipe passed down through the family; the owner is known to throw out an entire vat that doesn't meet standard. Fourteen versions of phở, plus bánh mì, bún thịt nướng, summer rolls and proper Vietnamese coffee.

Recommended byThe InfatuationFreshwater Cleveland+1

Cantonese. Freshwater calls it the best dim sum in Cleveland and it is still the only place running proper steel carts at weekend lunch — har gow, siu mai, chicken feet, roast duck, rice rolls, circling the room. Hundreds of people pack it on Saturday and Sunday.

Recommended byFreshwater ClevelandCleveland Magazine+1
4P
Must see· University Circle & East Side

Percy Skuy Collection, Dittrick Medical History Center

The largest and most comprehensive collection of historical contraceptives in the world — over a thousand objects arranged chronologically across a millennium. Beaver testicles, crocodile dung, mule earwax, spider carcasses, Mexican yams, Goodyear rubbers, IUDs. Alongside sit the texts: Casanova recommending a hollowed half-lemon, and 1580 BCE instructions involving ground dates, acacia and honey. The wider Dittrick galleries on medical history are excellent in their own right.

Recommended byAtlas ObscuraNews 5 Cleveland #28

280 acres, 100,000 graves, and the best architecture walk in the city: the James A. Garfield Memorial and tomb, Wade Memorial Chapel with a Louis Tiffany interior, and mausoleums throughout. Climb the Garfield tower for a downtown view.

Raymond Buckland — leader of what became the Long Island Coven — started this collection in a Bay Shore basement in the 1960s. How we choose

Recommended by name

Every place needs two credible named sources — or one Michelin or James Beard listing. Review sites can measure popularity; they never count as a recommender.

Pinned to the door

Each pin is read from an official site, OpenStreetMap or Wikipedia coordinates, graded for confidence and dated. No researched places still wait for a verified pin, so they are not shown yet.

Checked open — or flagged closed

Status is verified against a real source and dated. Closed places stay, clearly marked, so the record stays honest.
