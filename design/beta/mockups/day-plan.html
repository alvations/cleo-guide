    東京国立博物館
    Must see· Taitō-ku

    Tokyo National Museum東京国立博物館

    Japan's oldest and largest museum — 120,000 objects including 89 National Treasures, from Jōmon pottery to samurai armour and ukiyo-e, in Ueno Park.

    Recommended byWikipediaGo Tokyo+1

    119 m to the next stop, straight-line

  2. 2
    笹乃雪
    Must see· Taitō-ku · Tofu

    Sasanoyuki笹乃雪

    Tokyo's most famous tofu restaurant, founded in the Edo period by a tofu-maker brought from Kyoto by Kan'ei-ji's imperial abbot — silken tofu in sweet ankake sauce, by Uguisudani Station.

    Recommended byTime OutThe Japan Times

    249 m to the next stop, straight-line

  3. 3
    東京都美術館
    Worth the detour· Taitō-ku

    Tokyo Metropolitan Art Museum東京都美術館

    Kunio Maekawa's brick museum in Ueno Park hosting Tokyo's blockbuster special exhibitions and the big public art-society shows.

    Recommended byWikipediaGo Tokyo+1

    140 m to the next stop, straight-line

  4. 4
    上野動物園
    Worth the detour· Taitō-ku

    Ueno Zoo上野動物園

    Japan's oldest zoo (1882) inside Ueno Park — famous for its giant pandas, plus a five-storey pagoda left from Kan'ei-ji in the grounds.

    Recommended byWikipediaGo Tokyo+1

    412 m to the next stop, straight-line

  5. 5
    カヤバ珈琲
    Worth the detour· Taitō-ku · Kissaten

    Kayaba Coffeeカヤバ珈琲

    Kissaten in a 1916 Yanaka building, run as a coffee shop from 1938 to 2006 and reopened in 2009 with the original exterior and signage; known for its egg sandwich.

    Recommended byTime OutLonely Planet

    458 m to the next stop, straight-line

  6. 6
    谷中霊園
    Worth the detour· Taitō-ku

    Yanaka Cemetery谷中霊園

    Leafy old cemetery of shōguns' retainers and Meiji notables — cherry-tree avenues, the last shōgun Tokugawa Yoshinobu's tomb and the neighbourhood's resident cats.

    Recommended byWikipediaGo Tokyo
