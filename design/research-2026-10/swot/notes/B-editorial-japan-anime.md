# Competitive research B: travel/food discovery competitors + Japan and anime pilgrimage

Research date: 2026-10-10. Method: WebSearch only (WebFetch blocked by policy). About 40 searches.
Every claim links to a search-result URL. Where something wasn't found, the entry says so.
"(company claim)" marks figures that only appear in the company's own material. Search-result
summaries can paraphrase, so check any number on its source page before quoting it externally.

## 1. Atlas Obscura

- **Positioning:** a crowdsourced atlas of "hidden wonders": unusual places and foods, plus small-group trips and experiences. Audience: curious travellers and armchair explorers, skewing US. Sources: [webiano](https://webiano.digital/atlas-obscura-the-internets-cabinet-of-geographic-curiosities/), [podcast transcript](https://podcasts.happyscribe.com/get-together/crowdsourcing-the-world-s-hidden-wonders-jonathan-carey-atlas-obscura).
- **Business model:** advertising, Trips (its own and partner trips, e.g. a partnership with Intrepid), and a membership at **$8/month** whose main perk is a **$100 trip discount**, plus members-only events and early access. Sources: [join.atlasobscura.com/membership](https://join.atlasobscura.com/membership), [OpenJaw on Intrepid](https://openjaw.com/?p=184640).
  - Older coverage: trips and experiences were about 60% of revenue in one earlier year, and ads had fallen below 50%. Trip prices ranged from $20 to $5,000. Source: [Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/uncategorized/david-plotz-atlas-obscura-business-model/) (dated).
- **Scale:** revenue was **$18.3M in 2025, up from $15.6M in 2024**, with the **first annual profit, $2.6M, in its 16-year history**. Sources: [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/atlas-obscura-first-annual-profit/), [AO press](https://press.atlasobscura.com/atlas-obscura-featured-in-adweek-for-its-break-through-year-of-progress).
  - Database: more than 33,000 places and foods according to a third-party site ([webiano](https://webiano.digital/atlas-obscura-the-internets-cabinet-of-geographic-curiosities/)). Nearly 2,000 entries were added in 2024 ([AO](https://atlasobscura.com/articles/favorite-new-places-2024)).
- **Trust model:** users submit places, and staff editors review and edit them. Public contributor leaderboards rank people by places added or edited. Sources: [podcast transcript](https://podcasts.happyscribe.com/get-together/crowdsourcing-the-world-s-hidden-wonders-jonathan-carey-atlas-obscura), [user profile example](https://atlasobscura.com/users/movesbrit?view=added).
  - There is no fixed source-per-place standard and no open/closed verification process was found.
- **Freshness:** continuous community additions plus annual editorial roundups ([AO 2024](https://atlasobscura.com/articles/favorite-new-places-2024)).
- **Personalisation:** not found beyond saved lists and newsletters.
- **Distribution:** SEO-heavy place pages, newsletters, books and trips. No specific traffic figures were found.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - CEO Louise Story laid off 10 staff while the company was "on shaky financial footing". Co-founders Joshua Foer and Dylan Thuras were effectively pushed out, and shareholders voted them off the board. Source: [Semafor, Dec 2025](https://www.semafor.com/article/12/04/2025/staff-cuts-and-strategy-squabbles-split-atlas-obscura-leadership).
  - Editor-in-chief Nicholas Jackson and two other editorial staffers left in June 2025 ([Talking Biz News](https://talkingbiznews.com/?p=179383)).
  - Adweek notes the profit came partly from "a painful rash of reductions" ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/atlas-obscura-first-annual-profit/)).

**Sources:** join.atlasobscura.com/membership · adweek.com/media/atlas-obscura-first-annual-profit · press.atlasobscura.com · semafor.com (12/04/2025) · talkingbiznews.com/?p=179383 · technical.ly · openjaw.com/?p=184640 · webiano.digital · atlasobscura.com/articles/favorite-new-places-2024 · podcasts.happyscribe.com

## 2. Time Out (Time Out Group plc: Media + Time Out Market)

- **Positioning:** city "what's on" and "best of" editorial, plus branded food halls that physically curate the "best of the city" under one roof. Audience: urban locals and visitors, with a growing Gen Z share. Source: [Time Out AU](https://www.business.au.timeout.com/news/news/time-out-group-acquisition).
- **Business model:** Media (display ads, sponsored content, e-commerce and offers) and Markets (operated and licensed food halls). Source: [HL](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/stock-market-news/company--news/archive/time-out-takes-action-to-improve-profitability-as-earnings-slump).
- **Scale, FY to June 2025:**
  - Revenue £73.2M (prior year restated to £78.7M).
  - **Markets +9% to £46.7M; Media −26% to £26.6M.**
  - Adjusted EBITDA £7.1M; £35.1M impairment; pretax loss £58.8M.
  - Source: [HL](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/stock-market-news/company--news/archive/time-out-takes-action-to-improve-profitability-as-earnings-slump).
- **Scale, H1 to Dec 2025:** revenue +2% to £39.8M; Media back to £1.9M adjusted EBITDA; £8M equity placing. Sources: [Investegate interim results](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/time-out-group--tmo/interim-results/9499156), [Stockopedia RNS](https://www.stockopedia.com/share-prices/time-out-LON:TMO/news/interim-results-019e6a19-44e6-73ed-b7fb-1591e0ead468/).
- **Audience reach:**
  - Investor page (undated): **242M global monthly reach**, 108 cities in 39 countries ([timeout.com/investors](https://www.timeout.com/investors)).
  - Advertising pages: 333 cities in 59 countries ([TO HK advertising](https://timeout.com/hong-kong/advertising)).
  - December 2024 interview: 150M ([Magnetic](https://magnetic.media/news-views/highlights-insights-rob-biagioni-time-out)).
- **Trust model:** local staff editors and critics. Market vendors are hand-picked "best of the city".
- **Freshness:** daily and weekly editorial. **Personalisation:** not found.
- **Distribution:** SEO, social and newsletters, plus physical Markets as brand billboards.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Time Out blamed the media revenue slump on "audience shift to social media, growth of AI search and the US media industry slowdown" ([HL](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/stock-market-news/company--news/archive/time-out-takes-action-to-improve-profitability-as-earnings-slump)).
  - **Time Out Markets in Chicago and Boston closed on 23 Jan 2026**, citing inconsistent footfall from hybrid working and higher costs. The closures affected 19 vendors in Chicago and 15 in Boston, leaving only the New York Markets in the US. Sources: [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/operations/time-out-markets-are-closing-chicago-boston), [WTTW](https://news.wttw.com/2026/01/14/time-out-market-chicago-close-its-fulton-market-food-hall-next-week), [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/01/14/business/time-out-market-boston-closing/).
  - The interim results describe the Chicago closure and Boston licensing as part of the Markets restructuring ([Investegate](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/time-out-group--tmo/interim-results/9499156)).

**Sources:** hl.co.uk (earnings slump) · investegate.co.uk interim results · stockopedia.com RNS · timeout.com/investors · timeout.com/hong-kong/advertising · magnetic.media · business.au.timeout.com · restaurantbusinessonline.com · news.wttw.com · bostonglobe.com (2026/01/14)

## 3. Eater (Vox Media until 2026; now PMX / Penske Media)

- **Positioning:** food news and restaurant guides, best known for **editor-curated city maps**. Audience: restaurant-going urban diners. In 2025 it ran sites in 23 US cities plus a national site. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eater_(website)).
- **Ownership change:** Penske Media acquired Eater along with The Verge, SB Nation, Thrillist, Punch and others around **June 2026**. The brands sit in a new subsidiary called PMX, led by Ryan Pauley. Terms were not disclosed. Sources: [Variety AU](https://au.variety.com/2026/biz/news/pmc-vox-media-ryan-pauley-verge-eater-37803/), [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/media-others-news/penske-media-corporation-to-acquire-major-vox-media-brands-155581.html).
- **Business model:** advertising (Concert ad marketplace), affiliate links and reservations links. No subscription was found.
- **Core features:**
  - Maps (Essential 38, Heatmap) and an app with editor-curated maps, "browse lists and reserve tables", and more map pins. Sources: [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/eater/id6469005148?pt=541474), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eater_(website)).
  - The Heatmap is an editorial "what's new and what's hot" list updated monthly ([UT rhetoric-of-food, 2017](https://sites.dwrl.utexas.edu/rhetoric-of-food/?p=1255)).
- **Trust model:** staff and freelance editors and critics. No user reviews.
- **Freshness:** maps are refreshed periodically (Heatmap monthly). **Personalisation:** not found.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Vox laid off 15 Eater staff in August 2025, mostly at city sites, which the union says was about one-third of Eater union jobs and the second round that year. Some city sites, such as Eater Boston, were left with no employees. Sources: [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/08/14/business/eater-layoffs-food-journalism-future), [WBEZ](https://www.wbez.org/food-drink/2025/08/12/eater-layoffs-15-staffers-chicago-local-journalism), [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/reading/vox-lays-off-at-least-15-at-eater/), [D Magazine](https://www.dmagazine.com/food-drink/2025/08/eater-layoffs-deplete-an-already-weakened-dallas-food-media/).
  - The result is thin local freshness: maps for cities with no staff are hard to keep current.
- **Scale:** no traffic figures were found.

**Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eater_(website) · au.variety.com (PMC–Vox) · exchange4media.com · apps.apple.com Eater · sites.dwrl.utexas.edu · bostonglobe.com (2025/08/14) · wbez.org · niemanlab.org · dmagazine.com · mediapost.com/publications/article/408073

## 4. The Infatuation (JPMorgan Chase; includes Zagat)

- **Positioning:** a casual-voice restaurant guide organised around occasions ("Hit Lists", Best New Restaurants). Audience: millennial and Gen Z urban diners and, through Chase, affluent cardholders. Sources: [BU](https://www.bu.edu/articles/2019/the-infatuation-boston/), [yespress](https://yespress.io/the-infatuation).
- **Ownership:** **JPMorgan Chase announced the acquisition in September 2021**. The bank said The Infatuation "will retain its independent point of view and operate as a distinct brand" led by co-founder Chris Stang ([Chase media](https://media.chase.com/news/jpmorgan-chase-acquire-leading-restaurant-discovery-platform)). Zagat was acquired from Google in 2018 ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/jpmorgan-chase-to-acquire-company-behind-zagat-guidebook/606394/)).
- **Business model:**
  - Strategic value to Chase as a card perk, plus ads and branded content.
  - **EEEEEATSCON** festival, launched in Los Angeles in 2017 ([BU](https://www.bu.edu/articles/2019/the-infatuation-boston/)).
  - **Sapphire Reserve "Exclusive Tables":** about 275 to 293 restaurants curated by The Infatuation and booked via OpenTable and the Visa Dining Collection, tied to a **$300/year dining credit** ($150 per half-year). Sources: [Infatuation SR page](https://www.theinfatuation.com/sapphire-reserve-exclusive-tables), [Chase benefits](https://account.chase.com/sapphire/benefits), [The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/news/chase-sapphire-reserve-dining-program/).
- **Core features:**
  - 0–10 rating scale. The free app (launched 2011) offers map and list views, filters and saved lists, plus city newsletters ([BU](https://www.bu.edu/articles/2019/the-infatuation-boston/)).
  - A plan to revive Zagat's 30-point scale using Zagat user reviews alongside editorial ([Skift](https://skift.com/?p=2701)). Launch status is unconfirmed.
- **Trust model:** staff critics, with Zagat crowd ratings as a supplement.
- **Personalisation:** filters and saved lists. No algorithmic personalisation was found.
- **Distribution:** SEO, Instagram (#EEEEEATS) and the Chase cardholder channel.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Structural conflict of interest: the curator of a bank's card perk also reviews restaurants. **No published criticism or controversy was found in these searches**, so this is an inference, not a cited criticism.
  - The Points Guy noted the Chase program lacked Resy-style last-minute cancellation alerts at the time ([TPG](https://thepointsguy.com/news/chase-sapphire-reserve-dining-program/)).
- **Scale:** not found.

**Sources:** media.chase.com · bankingdive.com · theinfatuation.com/sapphire-reserve-exclusive-tables · account.chase.com/sapphire/benefits · thepointsguy.com · bu.edu · skift.com/?p=2701 · yespress.io

## 5. Michelin Guide (restaurants + MICHELIN Keys hotels via Tablet)

- **Positioning:** the institutional gold standard. Anonymous professional inspectors award Stars, Bib Gourmand and, since 2024, Keys for hotels. Audience: affluent and food-motivated travellers.
- **Business model:**
  - **Destination deals:** tourism boards pay to bring the guide to a region. Examples: California paid $600K in 2019 (the first such deal), Florida about $1.5M over 3 years, Texas cities $2.7M over 3 years. Sources: [Wasserstrom](https://www.wasserstrom.com/blog/2025/11/25/should-we-invest-in-the-michelin-guide/), [Tampa Bay Times](https://tampabay.com/life-culture/food/2024/04/15/michelin-guide-florida-everything-you-need-know-about-how-it-works/).
  - **Hotel bookings via Tablet:** Michelin acquired Tablet Hotels. At the 2023 Keys launch it said hotels would pay **10–15% commission** on bookings, with editorial and sales kept separate. Sources: [Washington Post via HANYC](https://hanyc.org/the-washington-post-michelin-guide-to-add-hotel-key-ratings-in-2024/), [Robb Report HK](https://robbreport.hk/posts/the-michelin-guide-will-start-rating-hotels-next-year).
  - Also licensing and events (not detailed in results).
- **Scale:** the first global Key selection (8 Oct 2025) named **2,457 hotels from more than 7,000 evaluated** ([Malay Mail / Media OutReach](https://www.malaymail.com/news/money/mediaoutreach/2025/10/09/malaysia-joins-the-michelin-guides-global-hotel-excellence-with-michelin-key-distinctions/417160)).
- **Trust model:** anonymous inspectors, with no user reviews. Critics say the opacity makes the process hard to evaluate ([Culinary Arts Switzerland](https://www.culinaryartsswitzerland.com/en/news/michelin-star-history/)).
- **Freshness:** annual selections per region. **Personalisation:** limited (app filters).
- **Distribution:** the brand halo, PR around annual ceremonies, SEO and app.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - **Pay-to-play criticism:** a Boston Globe critic called the destination model a "pay-to-play system". Virginia declined to pay, citing "responsible stewardship of taxpayer dollars". Boston's tourism board also refused. Michelin's position is that tourism boards fund "communication, digital and marketing campaigns". Sources: [Boston Globe](https://bostonglobe.com/2025/05/15/business/michelin-guide-star-tourism-boston-restaurants/), [Axios DC](https://www.axios.com/local/washington-dc/2025/04/14/michelin-guide-dc-star-restaurants-virginia-declines?stream=top), [Axios Boston](https://www.axios.com/local/boston/2025/02/20/why-boston-has-no-michelin-star-restaurants-meetboston-chefs).
  - **Coverage gaps:** inspector capacity is thin, and the guide skews to high-end dining ([Wasserstrom](https://www.wasserstrom.com/blog/2025/11/25/should-we-invest-in-the-michelin-guide/)).
  - **Pressure and bias:** the Bernard Loiseau and Sébastien Bras cases, and an alleged French or Eurocentric bias. Sources: [KRDO](https://krdo.com/news/2024/02/10/michelin-guide-history-how-did-a-tire-company-become-an-elite-restaurant-rating-guide/), [Culinary Arts Switzerland](https://www.culinaryartsswitzerland.com/en/news/michelin-star-history/). These sources are weaker.
  - **Hotel commissions:** taking booking commission from the hotels it rates invites the same conflict-of-interest questions ([HANYC/WaPo](https://hanyc.org/the-washington-post-michelin-guide-to-add-hotel-key-ratings-in-2024/)).

**Sources:** wasserstrom.com · tampabay.com · bostonglobe.com (2025/05/15) · axios.com (DC & Boston) · hanyc.org · robbreport.hk · malaymail.com (Media OutReach 2025/10/09) · journaldespalaces.com · culinaryartsswitzerland.com · krdo.com

## 6. Culture Trip (cautionary tale)

- **Positioning:** a millennial-focused "culture" travel media site that pivoted to hotel bookings and then to small-group tours. Sources: [Sky/Yahoo](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/culture-trip-embarks-journey-us-113300123.html), [bebeez](https://bebeez.eu/2023/07/12/culture-trip-millennial-focused-tour-operator-completes-mbo-and-launches-strategic-review/).
- **History:**
  - 2018 Series B of about $80M ([OMR](https://omr.com/de/daily/culture-trip-funding/)).
  - Around 1,500 articles a month from about 300 freelancers plus in-house staff ([WTM](https://hub.wtm.com/blog/social-media/2019-the-year-of-culture-trip)).
  - 2020 pandemic redundancies affected under half of London staff ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/05/04/culture-trip-confirms-layoffs.html)).
  - July 2023 management buyout and strategic review ([TravelMole](https://travelmole.com/news/culture-trip-completes-management-buyout)).
  - **February 2024: website and tour business sold to U.S. News & World Report after raising about $155M**, terms undisclosed ([Skift](https://skift.com/2024/02/29/culture-trips-final-pivot-sells-to-u-s-news-after-raising-155-million/amp/)).
  - There was no administration filing in 2023. The "collapse" was a down-exit.
- **Business model history:** ads, then hotel bookings (to compete with Meta and Google), then tours ([Yahoo/Sky](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/culture-trip-embarks-journey-us-113300123.html)).
- **Trust model:** local freelance contributors and editors, optimised for SEO ([OMR](https://omr.com/en/daily/culture-trip-bucks-trend)).
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Glassdoor complaints of "quantity over quality".
  - A trade critic called one piece a "clumsy SEO grab" ([WTM](https://hub.wtm.com/blog/social-media/2019-the-year-of-culture-trip)).
  - Reviewers flagged repetitive and duplicated content and questionable accuracy ([PilotPlans](https://www.pilotplans.com/blog/culture-trip-review)).
  - Lesson: SEO volume without verification or merit is not defensible.

**Sources:** skift.com (2024/02/29) · cnbc.com (2020/05/04) · travelmole.com · bebeez.eu · omr.com · hub.wtm.com · pilotplans.com · malaysia.news.yahoo.com

## 7. Lonely Planet (Red Ventures)

- **Positioning:** the legacy guidebook brand, now "digital-first". CEO Paul Yanover. Products include an AI assistant built on its own guide content, a new app with bookable experiences, and **Lonely Planet Journeys** (fall 2025: customised trips to 70+ destinations). Lonely Planet claims to be the number-one English-language travel publisher, with 770 titles in print (company claim). Source: [Breaking Travel News](https://www.breakingtravelnews.com/news/article/lonely-planet-unveils-new-app-and-bookable-experiences-as-it-evolves-to-a-d/).
- **Ownership:** **Red Ventures (owner of The Points Guy) bought it from NC2 Media in late 2020**. Skift estimated the price at about $50M. Sources: [Skift](https://skift.com/?p=412038), [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industrynews/publisher-news/article/85029-lonely-planet-acquired-by-red-ventures.html).
  - A Skift report (undated in results) says the parent was pursuing a sale ([Skift staging](https://skiftstaging.dev/?p=285637)).
  - **No 2025 sale was found.**
- **Business model:** print books, ads and affiliate (Red Ventures' performance-marketing model), bookable experiences and trips, app subscription.
  - The older "Guides" app moved behind a paywall at **$40/year** according to a user review ([justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1045791869/guides-by-lonely-planet/reviews)).
- **Trust model:** commissioned expert authors and editors.
- **Freshness:** print editions are slow to refresh.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - 2020: 70–80 roles cut weeks after the sale, with reports that editorial staff (writers, editors, cartographers) were let go ([The Bookseller](https://www.thebookseller.com/news/job-losses-lonely-planet), [Publishers Marketplace](https://lunch.publishersmarketplace.com/2020/04/status-major-layoffs-at-lonely-planet-more-agency-pay-cuts-wiley-reports-modest-impact)).
  - **June 2024: closed its China office** and stopped updating its Chinese social channels ([Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1015405/Lonely%20Planet%20Reaches%20the%20End%20of%20the%20Road%20in%20China), [Dao Insights](https://daoinsights.com/news/lonely-planet-exits-china-sparking-nostalgia-among-netizens/)).
  - **App shutdowns:** I could not confirm a specific Lonely Planet app shutdown in these searches. Several searches returned only the Google Trips shutdown, the 2017 "Trips" app launch, and repeated app relaunches. Treat this as not verified.

**Sources:** breakingtravelnews.com · skift.com/?p=412038 · publishersweekly.com · skiftstaging.dev/?p=285637 · thebookseller.com · lunch.publishersmarketplace.com · sixthtone.com · daoinsights.com · justuseapp.com · livreshebdo.fr (2017 Trips app)

## 8. Spotted by Locals

- **Positioning:** city guides written only by "Spotters", people who live in the city and speak the language. Founded by Bart and Sanne van Poll ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotted_by_Locals)).
- **Audience:** independent "travel like a local" city-breakers, mostly Europe.
- **Business model:** paid app (the result labels these as App Store prices).
  - **$3.99 per city guide**, or a **$4.99 monthly membership** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id470967003)).
  - Website shows full-access plans of €19.99 and €39.99 and a €9.99 option ([pricing](https://www.spottedbylocals.com/https-www-spottedbylocals-com-pricing-free-register)).
  - Cities launching a guide have been asked to pay for a €6,000 ad campaign (per result summary).
- **Scale:** 85 cities and 503 Spotters (company claim). Sources: [spottedbylocals.com/app](https://spottedbylocals.com/app), [site](https://www.spottedbylocals.com/?p=5890).
- **Trust model:** vetted local individuals, with a "no tourist traps" ethos. **Spotter pay is not found.** Earlier coverage calls them volunteer locals ([Johnny Jet](https://johnnyjet.com/guidebook-spotted-by-locals/)).
- **Freshness / personalisation:** offline app and favourites. Details not found.
- **Weaknesses (inferred, no cited criticism found):** small scale (85 cities), a paywall, and dependence on one author per spot.

**Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotted_by_Locals · apps.apple.com/app/id470967003 · spottedbylocals.com pricing / app / ?p=5890 · johnnyjet.com

## 10. Japan Guide (japan-guide.com)

- **Positioning:** the comprehensive English-language Japan travel reference. Founded July 1996 by Swiss native Stefan Schauwecker as a hobby. Run from Japan since 2003 by japan-guide.com Co., Ltd. Sources: [japan-guide about](https://japan-guide.com/e/e440.html), [Gov-Online 2014](https://www.gov-online.go.jp/eng/publicity/book/hlj/html/201410/201410_06_en.html).
- **Business model:** advertising. Ads earned enough for Schauwecker to go full-time ([Gov-Online](https://www.gov-online.go.jp/eng/publicity/book/hlj/html/201410/201410_06_en.html)). Also offers ad and promotional packages to Japanese destinations and businesses ([honichi](https://honichi.com/news/2017/06/14/inboundmediajapanguidecom/)).
- **Core features:** more than a thousand pages covering sights, accommodation and living in Japan, a user forum, and seasonal reports. A small team of about 5 native-English writers and editors visits places firsthand. Sources: [Gov-Online](https://www.gov-online.go.jp/eng/publicity/book/hlj/html/201410/201410_06_en.html), [Tomodachi 2017](https://japan.go.jp/tomodachi/2017/spring-summer2017/guiding_japan_visitors.html).
- **Trust model:** firsthand staff visits, plus forum Q&A.
- **Scale:** the forum drew 8–10M page views a month from more than 200 countries (2017 inbound-media listing) ([honichi](https://honichi.com/news/2017/06/14/inboundmediajapanguidecom/)). No current monthly-visitor figure was found.
- **Weaknesses (inferred, no cited criticism found):** a tiny team, so freshness is limited to the mainstream circuit. Little coverage of food or anime niches and no source-per-place attribution.

## 11. Tabelog (Kakaku.com)

- **Positioning:** Japan's dominant restaurant review, ranking and reservation site, using a crowd 5-point score with a weighting algorithm.
- **Scale:**
  - **About 100M monthly users, about 890,000 establishments, more than 85M reviews and photos** (company release, December 2025) ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251221963753/en)).
  - About 93.5M monthly users in March 2024 ([Bernama](https://mrem.bernama.com/viewsm.php?idm=53154)).
  - Launched an inbound reservation service for overseas visitors (company release) ([Bernama](https://mrem.bernama.com/viewsm.php?idm=48877)).
- **Business model:**
  - Restaurant listing and promotion fees, reservation fees, ads.
  - Premium user subscription, launched 2010: ¥315/month in 2013 and ¥390/month in 2014. The current price was not found. Sources: [The Bridge 2013](https://thebridge.jp/en/2013/07/tabelog-premium-members), [The Bridge 2014](https://thebridge.jp/en/2014/03/tabelog-350k).
- **Trust model:** user reviews weighted by an undisclosed algorithm.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - **Hanryumura lawsuit:** the yakiniku chain said all 21 of its outlets' scores dropped at once in May 2019.
    - **Tokyo District Court (June 2022):** ordered Kakaku.com to pay **¥38.4M**, finding the algorithm change an abuse of dominant bargaining position under antimonopoly law ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2022/06/algorithm-change-violates-japanese-antitrust-law), [Jiji](https://sp.m.jiji.com/english/show/20336)).
    - **Tokyo High Court (19 Jan 2024):** reversed the ruling in Kakaku.com's favour ([Diario Constitucional](https://www.diarioconstitucional.cl/2024/01/29/sitio-web-de-resenas-de-restaurantes-no-debera-indemnizar-a-cadena-de-restaurantes-por-cambios-en-sus-algoritmos-de-calificacion-resuelve-un-tribunal-japones/), [Business Insider JP](https://www.businessinsider.jp/article/255834/)).
    - **Supreme Court:** a wiki-style source reports a decision not to accept the appeal on 5 March 2026 (令和8年3月5日 上告不受理) ([scrapbox](https://scrapbox.io/jcomp/%E9%9F%93%E6%B5%81%E6%9D%91%E5%AF%BE%E3%82%AB%E3%82%AB%E3%82%AF%E3%82%B3%E3%83%A0%E9%A3%9F%E3%81%B9%E3%83%AD%E3%82%B0)). This is from a single, non-authoritative source.
  - **Paid-review scandal (January 2012):** Kakaku.com admitted ranking manipulation by 39 "yarase" agents selling planted reviews ([Asiajin](https://asiajin.com/blog/2012/01/tabelog-japans-yelp-reportedly-tainted-by-undercover-positive-review-agents/)).
  - A Japanese-only UX limited foreign usability until the recent inbound push (inference).

## 13. Anime pilgrimage (seichi junrei) tools

### 13a. Butaimeguri (舞台めぐり)
- **Basics:** an app from a Sony group company, live since 2013. Features AR "same composition" shots and photos with characters, location-limited AR characters, and sound-AR content (e.g. Seibu Railway's *Mienai Kizuna*, 2019). Sources: [ASCII](https://ascii.jp/elem/000/001/117/1117450/), [Dengeki](https://dengekionline.com/sp/mienaikizuna/).
- **Scale:** 80 titles as of August 2017. **Downloads not found.**
- **Model:** official tie-ups with rights-holders, railways and municipalities. Japanese-first.

### 13b. Anime Tourism Association: "88 Anime Spots" (訪れてみたい日本のアニメ聖地88)
- **Basics:** an annual selection since 2018 that local governments promote. The 2024 edition had **87 titles, 26 facilities, 2 events and 107 municipalities** ([Dengeki / ASCII via search](https://ascii.jp/elem/000/004/183/4183927/)).
- **2025 edition:** an example is Gunma's *Nanare Hananare* ([Gunma Pref.](https://www.pref.gunma.jp/site/houdou/685738.html)).
- **2026 edition:** expanded to **100 titles** for the association's 10th anniversary ([Sakigake](https://www.sakigake.jp/news/article/20260616PR0037/), [Koto City](https://www.city.koto.lg.jp/103000/contents_tourism/anime88-2026.html)).
- **Trust model:** institutional. Selection involves fan votes plus the rights-holder and municipality, giving official certification.
- **Weakness:** a list, not a navigable verified map. Its English UX was not assessed.

### 13c. Anitabi
- **Basics:** a free web and iPad app run by a Chinese developer, with no login required. Pins mark filming or scene locations. Users submit locations by long-pressing coordinates, then moderators review them. Comparison images are user-contributed. Content is licensed **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**. Sources: [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6745898838), [MoneyHero HK](https://www.moneyhero.com.hk/blog/zh/專欄文章/日本-blog-動畫巡禮-anitabi探索動漫與現實交匯的聖地巡禮地圖).
- **Weakness:** coordinate accuracy depends on crowd submissions plus moderation. The rights status of anime screenshots is unclear (inference).

### 13d. Seichi Junrei Map (聖地巡礼マップ, seichimap.jp)
- **Basics:** the business transferred from dip Corp. to Origress Inc. and relaunched on **1 August 2025**, aiming to become a "comprehensive anime experience media" ([AnimeAnime / PR Times](https://animeanime.jp/release/prtimes/20250801/241684.html), [ASCII](https://ascii.jp/elem/000/004/310/4310212/)).

## 14. Market context: Japan inbound and anime tourism

1. **2025 inbound arrivals:** **42.68M, up 15.8% year on year**, the first time above 40M ([Nissyoku](https://news.nissyoku.co.jp/news/yokotah20260121074409551), [Honichi](https://honichi.com/news/2026/01/27/weekmatome_20260127/)).
   - **2024:** 36.87M ([Skift](https://skift.com/2024/10/17/japan-tourism-sets-yet-another-record-as-visitor-spending-exceeds-last-years-high/), [The Traveler](https://www.thetraveler.org/japan-tourism-smashes-2025-records-as-visitors-and-spending-surge/)).
2. **2025 visitor spending:** about **¥9.5 trillion (¥9.4559T preliminary), up 16.4%**, or ¥229K per person ([Honichi](https://honichi.com/news/2026/01/27/weekmatome_20260127/)).
   - **2024:** ¥8.14T ([The Traveler](https://www.thetraveler.org/japan-tourism-smashes-2025-records-as-visitors-and-spending-surge/)).
   - **Government target:** 60M visitors and ¥15T by 2030 ([The Traveler](https://www.thetraveler.org/japan-tourism-smashes-2025-records-as-visitors-and-spending-surge/)).
3. **Anime-motivated share:** the share of foreign visitors who went to film or anime-associated places **rose from 4.6% in 2019 to 7.5% in 2023**. This is a Japan Tourism Agency survey figure, cited by Trip.com, not verified at the primary source ([Travel Tomorrow](https://traveltomorrow.com/anime-tourism-boom-drives-travel-demand-across-asia-trip-com-data-shows/)).

5. **Market size:** **no reliable Japan-specific anime tourism market figure was found.**
   - Proxies: the anime industry was about ¥3.3T in 2023 ([WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/14a5jrk-japanese-anime-industry-sales-top-trillion-yen-for)).
   - The global "anime tourism market" was $1.8B in 2025 according to an unverified market-research report ([MarketIntelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/anime-tourism-market)). This is low-credibility.

## Cross-cutting takeaways for SWOT

- **No competitor publishes per-place source attribution plus verified coordinates plus open/closed status.**
  - Editors' brands (Eater, Infatuation, Time Out, CNT) depend on staff headcount, which is shrinking: Eater, Condé Nast and Atlas Obscura all had layoffs.
  - Crowd platforms (Tabelog, Atlas Obscura, Anitabi) face manipulation and accuracy risk: Tabelog's paid-review agents, and its algorithm fight that went through district court, appeal and reportedly the Supreme Court.
  - Institutions (Michelin, the Anime Tourism Association 88) carry the most trust but face pay-to-play and coverage-gap critiques.
- **Threats:** AI search and social are taking discovery traffic. Time Out explicitly blamed AI search for its media revenue decline.
- **Opportunity:** the anime pilgrimage tools are fragmented (official AR app, annual list, crowd map, relaunched portal) and Japanese-first or Chinese-run. That leaves space for an English, source-traceable, verified-pin layer.
