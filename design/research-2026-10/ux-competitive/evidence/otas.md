# OTA & metasearch digital UX (2023–2026): evidence file

Method: about 30 WebSearch queries (standard mode), run 2026-10-10. WebFetch was blocked, so every claim below comes from the search-result summary for the linked URL and not from reading the page. **Unknown** means the searches turned up nothing. Fill those gaps with first-hand app teardowns. Many official sources are paywalled (Baymard Premium) or were not indexed (Booking BUI, Expedia UDS docs).

## Booking.com
- Visual language: brand blue `#003580` plus accent "signal" yellow `#febb02`. The type is a system stack (BlinkMacSystemFont/Inter) at dense, smaller sizes. These are **unofficial** third-party token extractions, not BUI docs — [webdesignhot Booking.com design.md](https://www.webdesignhot.com/design.md/booking-com/)
- The site is tagged "light · marketplace · travel · sans · dense · commercial · booking-funnel" (unofficial) — [webdesignhot booking design.md](https://www.webdesignhot.com/design.md/booking/)
- Design system: the official BUI documentation did **not** surface. Its public component and token specs are unknown — same search as above.
- UX writing: the team publishes the Medium blog **B.Writes** (medium.com/booking-writes). It is a community of more than 70 writers inside a UX organisation of about 200, and copy is translated into 44+ languages — [UX Writer II listing](https://designproject.io/jobs/ux-writer-ii-organic-marketing-at-booking-com-bq8pr1); [podcast w/ Booking content designer on accessibility](https://nhac.vn/podcast/making-digital-accessibility-a-priority-with-jaime-walke-booking-yuval-keshtcher-pca4Mxr)

It also lists ARIA roles, keyboard operability, consistent headings and landmarks, and clear error messages — [Accessibility Statement](https://www.booking.com/content/accessibility_statement.en-us.html)
- Map view, critiqued (iOS, 2024): the neighbourhood facility icons (transit, café) cannot be tapped and give poor feedback. The critique proposes tappable icons with a detail bottom panel, as Airbnb and Trip do — [Pratt IxD critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2024/09/design-critique-booking-com-ios-app/)
- App-store review analysis credits map view with making comparison easy, and Genius pricing with perceived value — [unitQ Epic Awards](https://epicawards.unitq.com/winners/booking-com)
- Genius is a loyalty tier giving 10–20% discounts and early access to deals. A 2026 teardown says the landing hero does not surface it — [Landing Doctor teardown](https://landingdoctors.com/teardowns/booking-com)
- Baymard has captured Booking.com search results and the mobile search field across 2021–2024. The annotations are paywalled — [Baymard SRP example](https://baymard.com/ecommerce-design-examples/33-search-results-page/19354-booking-com); [Baymard mobile search field](https://baymard.com/ecommerce-design-examples/32-search-field/10327-booking-com)
- AI Trip Planner: a beta launched 28 Jun 2023 for US app users, built on in-house recommendation models plus the ChatGPT API. You refine the search in chat, and results come back as a visual list of destinations and properties with prices that link straight into the booking flow. It later expanded to the UK, AU, NZ and SG, with AI review summaries planned — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/ai-travel-planner-bookingcom-to-use-chatgpt-to-help-plan-itineraries-book-tickets-387352-2023-06-28); [Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4124415/bookingcom-enhances-travel-planning-with-new-ai-powered-features-for-easier-smarter-decisions); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/booking-com-launches-chatgpt-powered-ai-travel-planner/?amp=1)
- Card anatomy, first-screen hierarchy, skeletons and sticky CTA: **unknown**. No accessible source covered them.

## Trip.com
- **Trip.Best** is a curated ranking with four sections (stays, things to do, nightlife, restaurants). The first destination was Thailand (June 2022, hotels). Lists are scored on quality, popularity, reputation and standout factor, using AI over ratings, reviews and search and booking data plus expert input. Sources say only the top 1% is featured and that lists update monthly — [Time Out HK](https://www.timeout.com/hong-kong/travel/plan-an-unforgettable-trip-overseas-with-trip-bests-recommendations); [Marketech APAC](https://marketech-apac.com/?p=56168); [Business Today TW](https://www.businesstoday.com.tw/article/category/183031/post/202507290040/)

- The Pratt critique of Booking's map cites Trip as having tappable POIs with info sheets (a positive pattern) — [Pratt IxD](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2024/09/design-critique-booking-com-ios-app/)

## Baymard Institute (travel accommodations)
- 2026 benchmark: 590+ UX parameters. Only **1 "decent"** site and none "good" or better — [Baymard 2026 benchmark](https://baymard.com/blog/travel-accommodations-ux-benchmark-2026)
- 25–30% of sites don't make the booking search the main homepage content. 40% lack popular industry-specific filters. 85% don't always link to third-party reviews — [Baymard travel best practices](https://baymard.com/research-articles/travel-site-ux-best-practices); [booking search article](https://baymard.com/blog/travel-accommodations-booking-search)
- 70% of OTA and large-brand hotel sites default to a list without a map, and users miss the "Map View" link. Baymard recommends a **split view** (list + map), because exact location is key to judging relevance — [Baymard split view](https://baymard.com/research-articles/accommodations-split-view)
- 2022 study: Booking.com, Expedia, Trip.com, hotel chains, boutique hotels, Airbnb and Vrbo. It found 1,000+ issues and produced 364 guidelines — [Baymard new research](https://baymard.com/research-articles/new-research-travel-accommodations)
- Dense pins can be handled two ways: cluster them into areas at wide zoom, or cap how many individual pins show at each zoom. Entering map mode can disorient users — [ClickZ](https://clickz.com/?p=94783)

## Nielsen Norman Group
- **Bottom sheets** (2023) count as progressive disclosure that keeps context visible. Modal sheets use a scrim, and nonmodal ones expand to modal. The reachability benefit is "largely a myth". Support Back for dismissal, show a visible Close button, don't stack sheets, and don't use them for page-to-page flows. Keep them for short tasks — [NN/g Bottom Sheets](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/); [Accidental overlay dismissal](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/accidental-overlay-dismissal/)
- **Mobile faceted search**: a filter "tray" overlay keeps the results partly visible. On mobile, batch filters behind an **Apply** button — [NN/g mobile faceted search](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-faceted-search/)
- Show filter state with applied-filter chips, real-time counts and predictable persistence (course outline) — [NN/g Filters & Sorting course](https://www.nngroup.com/contents/self-paced-courses/filters-and-sorting-the-complete-design-guide/); [Filter categories & values](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/filter-categories-values/); [User intent affects filter design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/applying-filters/)
- **Maps on mobile**: a prominent map on results made choosing harder, and the map was hard to scroll past. Default to the **list** with a map toggle in apps, show distance in the list, and let users hide an embedded map. (This is an older article and pulls against Baymard's split view on desktop.) — [NN/g Mobile maps & location finders](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-maps-locations/)
- **Skeleton screens** reduce perceived load time — [NN/g video](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/skeleton-screens-vs-progress-bars-vs-spinners/). Evidence is mixed: a Viget 2017 study found skeletons did worse than spinners — [CyberOptik glossary](https://www.cyberoptik.net/glossary/skeleton-screen/)
- **Trust**: users trust testimonials from external sites more than reviews hosted on the site itself — [NN/g Trustworthy design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/); [Ecommerce product pages incl. reviews](https://www.nngroup.com/reports/ecommerce-ux-product-pages-including-reviews/)

## Recurring patterns
1. **The map–list relationship is the key layout decision.** Split view on wide screens (Baymard), list-first with a map toggle on phones (NN/g), and filters kept in sync between map and list (Kayak) — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/accommodations-split-view), [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-maps-locations/), [Koddi](https://koddi.com/blog/kayak-and-trivago-enhance-destination-search-experiences/)
2. **Tappable POIs on the map with a detail sheet** (Trip, Airbnb, and Kayak's POI layer). Inert icons are criticised — [Pratt](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2024/09/design-critique-booking-com-ios-app/)
3. **Pin density control**: cluster, or cap pins per zoom level — [ClickZ](https://clickz.com/?p=94783)
4. **Filters in a tray or bottom sheet with a batch Apply ("Show N results"), plus applied chips.** Quick "freebie" toggles (free cancellation, breakfast) go up top — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-faceted-search/), [Kayak/Koddi](https://koddi.com/blog/kayak-and-trivago-enhance-destination-search-experiences/)
5. **Search as the hero of the home screen** — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/travel-site-ux-best-practices)
6. **Saved lists you can collaborate on** (likes, comments, polls, invites) — [Expedia Trip Planner](https://www.expedia.com/cn/why/trip-planning), [Vrbo boards](https://www.rentalscaleup.com/expedia-vrbo-trip-boards/)
7. **Curated, methodology-backed rankings** used as a trust badge (Trip.Best: four criteria, monthly refresh) — [Time Out HK](https://www.timeout.com/hong-kong/travel/plan-an-unforgettable-trip-overseas-with-trip-bests-recommendations)
8. **Conversational / AI planners that hand off to structured cards and booking** (Booking AI Trip Planner, Romie, Kayak AI Mode, Google AI Mode Canvas beside a map) — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/ai-travel-planner-bookingcom-to-use-chatgpt-to-help-plan-itineraries-book-tickets-387352-2023-06-28), [Engine](https://engine.com/blog/inside-googles-travel-ai-upgrade-ai-mode-canvas-conversational-flight-deals-and-review-summaries-in-maps)
9. **Time-to-buy signals**: price calendars and forecasts (Hopper's colour calendar, Kayak's forecast arrow, Google alerts scoped to a map area) — [Pilot Plans](https://www.pilotplans.com/blog/hopper-review), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/01/15/book-now-or-wait-kayak-brings-price-forecasting-to-its-flight-search-results)
10. **Photo-forward browsing modes** (Agoda Gallery View) — [Agoda press](https://www.agoda.com/press/agoda-rolls-out-suite-of-new-features-focused-on-real-time-updates-and-smarter-trip-planning/)
11. **Token-based, multi-platform, themeable design systems** (Backpack light and dark across web, iOS and Android; Expedia with three brand themes on shared structure) — [Supernova](https://www.supernova.io/blog/top-design-system-documentation-sites-created-with-supernova), [Code and Theory](https://www.codeandtheory.com/work/expedia-group)
12. **Open-ended "Everywhere" or explore discovery** for users without a fixed destination — [FutureFive](https://futurefive.co.nz/story/skyscanner-releases-improved-version-flights-app)

## Anti-patterns (with regulatory or critical evidence)
- **Fake scarcity and pressure selling** ("only 1 room left", "X people looking"), misleading discounts, rankings skewed by commission, and hidden charges. In 2019 the CMA took enforcement action against Agoda, Booking.com, ebookers, Expedia, Hotels.com and Trivago, and they committed to fix these by 1 Sep 2019 — [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/travel/article/booking-com-misleading-sales-tactics-cma); [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/hotel-booking-sites-told-to-clean-up-their-act-on-deals-and-hidden-charges-aOPW73K3f8c6)
- Which? later found Booking.com's "only one room left on our site" claims inaccurate in 5 of 10 checks. In one case a single "secret deal" room was flagged while 34 rooms were free — [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/booking-com-still-misleading-holidaymakers-with-1-room-left-claims-aSNln2j8gkGl)
- EU CPC / Commission commitments (from June 2020): scarcity messages must say "only for Booking.com offers", show the final price, and disclose whether the host is a private or professional host. A 2025 Dutch mass claim alleges fake discounts and fabricated scarcity — [Brussels Times](https://brusselstimes.com/1063448/only-one-room-left-booking-com-reprimanded-for-misleading-consumers); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/travel%2Fonly-one-room-left-bookingcom-faces-massive-lawsuits-over-anti-competitive-practices-market-abuse-deceptive-pricing-fabricated-scarcity-tactics-1.500230124); [Gigazine](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20191223-booking-com-eu-comission)
- **Drip pricing and pre-selected add-ons**: in its $35M FTC settlement (July 2026), Hopper is alleged to have pre-selected "Tip" and VIP Support fees on a screen users had to scroll to see. The FTC also says it overstated what Price Freeze delivered: the freeze is capped and depends on availability — [Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/07/16/hopper-reaches-35-million-agreement-with-the-ftc/); [Covington Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/united-states/federal-trade-commission/ftc-settles-with-hopper-over-hidden-fee-practices/)
- **Map hidden behind a link** (70% of sites default to list only) — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/accommodations-split-view)
- **A full-width map users must scroll past on mobile** — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-maps-locations/)
- **Bottom sheets that are stacked, swipe-only to dismiss, or used as page flows** — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/)
- **Inert map icons** — [Pratt](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2024/09/design-critique-booking-com-ios-app/)
- **Overwhelming, unclustered pins** (Hotels.com example) — [ClickZ](https://clickz.com/?p=94783)
- **Only self-hosted reviews.** 85% of sites don't link to third-party reviews, and users trust external reviews more — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/travel-site-ux-best-practices); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)
