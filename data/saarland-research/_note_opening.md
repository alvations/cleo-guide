## Canon established (the spine to extend from)
- **SAAR food:** Saarland home kitchen — Schwenker/Schwenkbraten, Dibbelabbes, Gefillde, Lyoner, house-brewed
  beer — plus the Michelin bench (Bau 3★ Perl-Nennig; Erfort in the city). Anchors: Brauhaus zum Stiefel
  (Gefillde/Saarland-Teller), La Bastille (Dibbelabbes), Hilde und Heinz (modern).
- **MOSELLE food:** quiche lorraine, pâté lorrain, Munster/tomme de Moselle, mirabelle — represented by the
  well-sourced **Marché Couvert** (sight) + a Lorraine bistro (Le Bistrot de G) and the Michelin Magasin aux Vivres.
- **LUX food:** the national dishes — Judd mat Gaardebounen, Gromperekichelcher, Bouneschlupp, Quetschentaart,
  Rieslingspaschtéit — plus Crémant de Luxembourg / Moselle wine, plus the Michelin bench (Mosconi). Anchors:
  Um Dierfgen, Kaempff-Kohler, Domaines Vinsmoselle.
- **MOSEL food/drink:** steep-slate Riesling and the Weinstube — Weinstube Kesselstatt (Trier, Reichsgraf von
  Kesselstatt) and the Bernkasteler Doctor at Bernkastel-Kues.
- **Sights spine:** two UNESCO World Heritage anchors carry the guide — **Völklinger Hütte** (SAAR) and the
  **Roman Monuments of Trier** (MOSEL, list 367: Porta Nigra, Dom/Liebfrauen, Kaiserthermen, Konstantinbasilika,
  Amphitheater). Plus the **Luxembourg City old-town** UNESCO ensemble (Bock casemates, Corniche, Grund) and the
  Saarschleife natural icon.

## Notable finds
- **UNESCO:** Völklinger Hütte (1994, first industrial WHS); Roman Trier + Cathedral + Liebfrauenkirche (list 367);
  Luxembourg City fortifications & old town (list 699); Echternach Hopping Procession (Intangible Heritage).
- **Michelin:** Victor's Fine Dining by **Christian Bau** — 3★ since 2005, La Liste world top-10, at the exact
  DE/FR/LU tripoint (Perl-Nennig). **Klaus Erfort** (Saarbrücken, long 3★, currently 2★ per the live Michelin Guide
  — recorded accurately). **Mosconi** (Luxembourg, 1★ + 18/20 Gault&Millau). **Le Magasin aux Vivres** (Metz, 1★).
- **Craft/faïence twist unique to this region:** Villeroy & Boch's Alte Abtei (Mettlach) and the Sarreguemines
  Musée de la Faïence — two sides of the same Saar-Moselle ceramics story straddling the border.
